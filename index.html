Lucas's AI trading bot · live trial

Virtual money only · last update 2026-11-13 14:15 (Madrid) · updates every 15 minutes

I designed and trained an automated trading bot, built with Claude, that manages a virtual eToro account on its own. It has one year to show it can follow its rules and keep up with the market before I trust it with real money. This page updates itself every 15 minutes.

1

The rules trade

Twice every weekday, at the European open and again when the US opens, a program with no AI works through about 350 stocks and funds (US, Spain, Europe, Asia, commodities) and buys the ones that pulled back inside an uptrend, strongest first, each with a stop-loss that is live at the broker all day and rises as the trade gains. About 60% stays in an MSCI World index fund (about 1,400 companies) while the market's trend is up and moves to cash when it turns down; gold and government bonds get 10% each only while they trend up; idle cash sits in short-term US Treasury bills. Hard limits are enforced in code: no leverage, no short selling, 1.5% of the account at risk per trade (2% maximum), at most 15% in one position, and safety stops at −15% and −25%.

2

The AI supervises

Before the orders go out, Claude reads the day's news on each planned trade and can veto one for a concrete reason, such as earnings this week or a profit warning. It cannot pick trades or change the limits. A watchdog with no AI checks prices every 15 minutes and wakes Claude only for a shock. If the AI is unavailable, the rules still trade, so the bot never stalls and uses very little AI.

3

The testing

Every rule was tested before going live: 60+ years of S&P 500 data, MSCI World since 1979, 500 US stocks and the German DAX, crash tests from 1987 to 2022. The tests showed the profit comes from staying in uptrends, cutting losers early and letting winners run, not from clever entries, so since 11 November 2026 the tested rules place the trades themselves. Rules that didn't hold up on new data were dropped.

How it's judged: a fixed scorecard at 3 and 12 months. Does it follow its rules, keep losses within what the simulations predicted, and keep up with its benchmark, MSCI World (the S&P 500 and Spain's IBEX 35 are shown for reference)? Over weeks, results are mostly luck, so the verdict comes at month 12.
